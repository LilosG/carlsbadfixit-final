If you're planning a home project in Encinitas, you've probably wondered: should I hire a handyman or a general contractor? The answer depends on your project's scope, complexity, and budget. Let me break down the real differences so you can make the right call.

## The Core Difference: Scope and Licensing

A **general contractor** in California holds a state license (Class B General Building Contractor or specialty licenses) and is bonded and insured. They're required to pull permits, manage multiple trades, and handle projects of significant complexity and cost. They oversee the entire project from start to finish, coordinating subcontractors, inspections, and compliance.

A **handyman** typically operates without a state contractor's license and handles smaller repairs, installations, and maintenance tasks. In California, handymen can work on jobs up to a certain dollar threshold without a license, though regulations vary. They work independently or as part of a small team, and they don't pull permits or manage complex multi-trade projects.

The key distinction: a general contractor is licensed to manage and coordinate large, complex projects. A handyman is equipped to handle direct, hands-on work for smaller jobs.

## Project Scope: The Real Dividing Line

**Hire a handyman for:**
- Single-room updates (paint, flooring, fixtures)
- Furniture assembly and installation
- TV mounting and cable management
- Drywall patching and minor repairs
- Plumbing fixture replacement (faucets, toilets)
- Carpentry work like shelving or trim
- Honey-do lists and miscellaneous repairs

**Hire a general contractor for:**
- Full kitchen or bathroom remodels
- Structural changes (removing walls, adding rooms)
- Major electrical work (panel upgrades, rewiring)
- Plumbing system overhauls
- HVAC installation or replacement
- Foundation or roofing work
- Projects requiring multiple licensed trades

The boundary gets fuzzy in the middle. A smaller room remodel—say, updating a guest bedroom with new flooring, paint, and built-ins—might fall into handyman territory if it doesn't require permits. A larger remodel involving electrical upgrades, structural changes, or complex plumbing almost always needs a general contractor.

## Permitting and Code Compliance

Here's where licensing matters most. In Encinitas, projects involving electrical work, plumbing, structural changes, or HVAC typically require permits and inspections. A general contractor knows the local code requirements, pulls permits, and ensures inspections pass. They're liable if something goes wrong.

A handyman can't legally pull permits in California. If your project needs one and a handyman does the work without it, you're exposed to liability, insurance complications, and potential code violations. If you later sell your home, unpermitted work can surface during inspection and create legal headaches.

That said, many handyman jobs don't require permits. Replacing a faucet, painting a room, or installing shelving doesn't need a permit. A good handyman knows the difference.

## Cost: What You Actually Pay

Handymen charge hourly ($50–$100+ per hour depending on experience and the job) or flat rates for specific tasks. A day of work might run $400–$800.

General contractors typically charge per project or hourly at higher rates ($75–$150+/hour) because they carry overhead: licensing, bonding, insurance, crew management, and liability. A full kitchen remodel might cost $15,000–$50,000+.

The trade-off: you pay more upfront with a contractor, but you get licensed, permitted, insured work with accountability. With a handyman, you save money on smaller jobs but lose the legal protections and coordination that come with a licensed contractor.

## When a Handyman Can Handle a Remodel

Here's the nuance many homeowners miss: an experienced handyman can handle smaller remodels that don't require permits or multiple licensed trades. If you're updating a bedroom with new flooring, paint, custom shelving, and trim work, a skilled handyman can do it. If you're adding recessed lighting or upgrading plumbing, you need a licensed electrician or plumber—or a general contractor to coordinate them.

## Insurance and Liability

A licensed general contractor carries liability insurance and workers' compensation. If someone gets hurt on the job, the contractor's insurance covers it. If a mistake causes property damage, they're responsible.

A handyman may carry liability insurance (good ones do), but they're not required to. If something goes wrong—a wall gets damaged, an electrical outlet sparks—you might be liable. Always ask a handyman about their insurance before hiring.
